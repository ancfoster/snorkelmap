Add a new location to SnorkelMap

You're adding a location for everyone

Once saved, your entry goes live on SnorkelMap for the whole community to use. Snorkellers planning a trip, researchers, and conservation groups may all rely on what you share, so accuracy matters more than completeness.

Other snorkellers can add to and edit this location

A location is not yours alone once it is published. Other members can add photos, correct details, update hazard information and edit what you have written, in the same way as a shared reference site. Every change is recorded in the location's edit history, so nothing is lost and you can see who changed what.

This means you do not need to get everything right first time, and you do not need to fill in the parts you are unsure about. Someone who knows the site will fill in the gaps.

Only add what you know

Every field is optional except a name, a pin and one photo. A short, accurate entry is far more useful than a full one with guesses in it.

One photo is needed to publish

To publish a location you need at least one photo of the above water surroundings, so people can recognise the place when they arrive. Underwater photos are welcome but not required, and you can add them at any time later.

What makes a good entry

  • Descriptions in your own words, based on your own experience
  • Hazards, nature and things you have personally observed
  • Only fill in what you know, others will fill in any gaps

2. Basic Details

Use the name local people use, or a descriptive name if there isn't one. Avoid vague names like "Rocky beach".

e.g. Playa de la Malagueta, Backwash Bay

If there are other names people use for this place, add them here. This helps others find the location when they search for it.

Help fellow snorkellers understand what the location is like, above and below the water, and what to expect when they get there. Please mention anything worth cautioning others about: a current that picks up on a certain tide, a slippery exit, boat traffic through the bay, water quality after heavy rain. If something gave you pause, it is worth writing down.

How do people actually get in and out of the water? Steps, a slipway, a shingle beach, a scramble over boulders. Mention anything that changes with the tide or the conditions.
